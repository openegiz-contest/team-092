## Mine

SmartMine AI is a fictional underground copper mine in Kazakhstan.

The implemented digital twin represents one mine with environmental, ventilation, and safety features.

Digital twin ID: `smartmine.ai:copper-mine-01`

The twin includes:
- Temperature, methane concentration, and humidity
- Ventilation fan status and airflow
- Safety risk level
- Underground worker count

The mine is modeled as having three underground zones and a depth of 450 meters. These are fictional configuration values, not measured site data.

Implementation:
- `team/smartmine/twins.json` — digital twin definition
- `team/smartmine/setup_twins.py` — twin initialization
- `team/smartmine/simulator.py` — synthetic sensor data and safety rules

## What works and what does not

Verified:
- OpenEgiz platform starts with Docker Compose.
- The SmartMine digital twin is created in Eclipse Ditto.
- The Python simulator publishes synthetic measurements through MQTT.
- Telemetry is stored in InfluxDB.
- Grafana displays methane, temperature, risk level, and airflow.
- The simulator runs in a Docker container.
- `bash team/smartmine/start.sh` successfully starts the integrated prototype.

Not implemented:
- A trained machine-learning prediction model
- Physical mine sensor integration
- Interactive 3D mine visualization
- Manual scenario controls on the Stand
- Real-world safety validation

## Data and Assumptions

All operational data in this prototype is synthetic and generated by Python.

The mine location, depth, number of zones, worker count, and environmental measurements are fictional demonstration parameters.

Safety classification uses illustrative thresholds:

- HIGH: methane >= 1.0% or temperature >= 30°C
- MEDIUM: methane >= 0.5% or temperature >= 27°C
- LOW: otherwise

Ventilation airflow changes according to the simulator's rules.

These thresholds are demonstration assumptions, not certified industrial safety limits.

No confidential company data or personal employee data is used.

## Limitations

SmartMine AI is a proof-of-concept digital twin using simulated measurements.

The current safety rules are deterministic and are not trained machine-learning predictions. The prototype does not connect to physical sensors or control real ventilation equipment.

The system must not be used for operational mine safety decisions without engineering validation, certified instrumentation, and appropriate regulatory compliance.
